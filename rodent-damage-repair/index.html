Why mending comes last.

Because rodent teeth grow without stopping, the animals gnaw to wear them down, and they gnaw to open a path. Insulation becomes bedding, duct wrap turns into nest material and wallboard is gnawed to widen a gap. After the animals are gone, the damage remains and often needs its own plan.

Doing repair first is a mistake. If the entry points are open, new animals return and chew the new material. That is why the work follows trapping and sealing.

What usually needs fixing.

  • Attic insulation chewed into runs or compressed into nests.
  • Duct wrap and flexible ducts with tears or gnaw holes.
  • Window and vent screens with holes at the edges.
  • Drywall corners and baseboard trim gnawed to widen a gap.
  • Plastic and rubber items such as hoses, bags and seals.

Cable and wire jackets are a special case. Chewed insulation on electrical wire is a fire risk and should be left to an electrician. If you see bare copper, do not touch it, and keep power use to a minimum until it is checked.

How a repair visit works.

The technician reviews the inspection notes, opens areas that need a closer look and lists the damage. You receive a written scope with materials and cost for each item, so you can approve repairs in stages.

Keeping records.

Photograph the damage before anything is covered and keep the written scope. If your insurer asks for documentation, those records help. The inspection page explains how the first quote is prepared.

Before the truck is in your driveway.

  • Open a route to the back of the property and mention any loose dog.
  • Leave shrubs and vines against the wall as they stand until we have looked.
  • Bring a small torch for dim corners.
  • Take photos of burrows, droppings and bits of nest before the weather moves them.

What to do while you wait

These lines boil down published health guidance, and they stay true even if you never book a visit.

  • Rule one for droppings: nothing dry. A broom or a shop vacuum sends old waste airborne.
  • Weeks of stillness behind a closed door call for a good cross-breeze.
  • Leave gloves on until the job is done, then remove them inside out and wash.
  • Spray until glossy, then walk away briefly.
  • Bag it: towels, droppings and any dead animal, picked up with tongs or a gloved hand.
  • Not feeling well after contact with droppings is a question for a physician.

Repair questions.

What is the right moment for repairs?

Once trapping and sealing are done. Anything fixed while animals remain gets gnawed again.

Do you fix electrical wiring?

We note chewed wire and recommend an electrician. Keep clear of anything that looks bare.

Will insurance cover rodent damage?

Your policy decides. Ask your insurer, and hold on to photos and the written estimate.

Can ducts be cleaned instead of replaced?

Light contamination may be treated, but chewed or torn ducts usually need patching or replacement.

How do I know how much damage there is?

The inspection finds visible damage and flags hidden areas. Some damage appears only when insulation or drywall is opened.
