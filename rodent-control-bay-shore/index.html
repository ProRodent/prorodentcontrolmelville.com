What triggers a call.

Few callers phone in because of a sighting. More often it is something like:

  • A pantry box with a hole in the side.
  • Noises that seem to move from the wall to the ceiling.
  • Tracks in dust on a basement shelf or the attic floor.
  • Dark oily rubs on a baseboard or pipe.

Weather signals worth watching.

Summer brings its own pattern. Garden crops, bird seed and pet bowls draw rodents toward a house, and from the yard it is a short step to a gap.

Leaf drifts and brush piles give shelter all year, but they matter most just before the cold. Moving them away from the wall removes a covered path.

Sending a technician to Bay Shore.

Middle distances need a little planning. For Bay Shore, the dispatcher looks at the day's other bookings before suggesting a time.

Our routine and its reasons.

The visit begins with a slow walk around the outside, looking at the roof edge, vents, pipe entries, garage door and foundation. Then the technician goes inside to the attic, basement or whichever space has signs.

Trapping follows the evidence. Traps go along the walls and routes where droppings and grease smears show the animals travel, matched to mice or rats and kept away from children and pets.

Facts that steer the first walk-through.

To place the first trap line well, we want to hear about the building. Callers in Bay Shore can mention:

  • A shed, deck or porch that sits right against the foundation.
  • Recent roofing, siding, plumbing or landscaping work.
  • A compost bin or brush pile close to a door.
  • Hedges, vines or branches that touch the siding or roof.

Rodents dig in quickly, and calling early generally runs cheaper than patching damage after weeks of waiting.
