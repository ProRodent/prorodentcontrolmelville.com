When the first trouble shows up.

Summer brings its own pattern. Garden crops, bird seed and pet bowls draw rodents toward a house, and from the yard it is a short step to a gap.

Walk the outside of the house in early fall and again after the first hard freeze. Look for fresh soil, loosened covers and leaves piled against siding.

Ahead of the technician's arrival.

  • Keep gnawed cable, nest scraps and droppings out of reach of toddlers and dogs.
  • Open a shed or garage that has sat closed for weeks and let it breathe before you enter.
  • Leave any trap you already set exactly where it sits.
  • Mention any storm debris work, roofing or leaf clearing from recent weeks.

Lungs first

Tidying before a visit is fine if you keep to this sequence and stop whenever a step is unclear.

  • Filters leak at the microscopic level, so even a good vacuum gets left in the closet.
  • Closed rooms keep their air, so let a shut-up space exchange it before you walk in.
  • Keep gloved hands away from eyes, nose and mouth until they are off and washed.
  • Contact time matters with disinfectant, so apply plenty and resist the urge to hurry.
  • When the bag is knotted and binned, wash your hands one more time.
  • If you feel strange later, mention the cleanup to your doctor.
