One detail belongs at the top. Melville has two ZIP codes in the coverage file, 11747 and 11760. The second has a blank city column and was matched by its distance from the first. Neither appears in the table below, which lists only the surrounding places.

Villages, hamlets and counted places.

Long Island is cut into towns, each holding villages that are legally incorporated and hamlets that are not. Villages on this list include Northport, Farmingdale, Lindenhurst, Amityville, Bayville, Islandia, Roslyn, Mineola, Williston Park, Garden City, Sea Cliff, Freeport, Old Westbury and the Village of Hempstead. Nearly every other entry is a hamlet with no municipal government, which changes nothing about how we trap rodents or seal a house.

Suffolk County provides the Huntington, Smithtown, Islip and Babylon sides of the list, and Nassau County the Oyster Bay, Hempstead and North Hempstead sides. A mailing address can name a village even when the lot is in a hamlet, so ZIP code is our test.

Where the data is thin.

Some rows in the coverage file have no city name. That applies to a second Hicksville code, one Great Neck code, four Port Washington codes and a few others, and the individual pages say so. For those, a street address is the final check.

Distance and range, answered.

How is distance counted?

Distance means a straight line between the center points of two ZIP codes. Real roads are longer, which is why dispatch quotes a time window by phone.

What if my code is absent from the table?

Phone with your street all the same. A few codes sit just beyond our reach, and dispatch gives you a plain answer on whether a visit is possible.

Why are Melville's own ZIP codes not in the table?

Because Melville is our base, it gets no row of its own. Its codes, 11747 and 11760, are in range by definition.

Does each page cover a whole village?

Each page lists just the ZIP codes that passed our checks. A village or hamlet can span more codes than we hold, so what is printed on your mail settles it.
