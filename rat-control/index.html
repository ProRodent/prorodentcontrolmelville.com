Why the yard is first.

Norway rats live close to the ground, tunneling next to slabs, footings and the bottom of steps and using the same worn lanes every night. When one reaches a wall cavity, a trail normally already exists outside, so the walk begins in the grass.

Their draws are quite ordinary: seed dropped under a feeder, a dripping hose bib, a compost heap, firewood stacked on siding. None of these says a household is unclean. They are simply what a rat samples while following a fence line.

Rats versus mice.

Droppings give the cleanest answer. Rat pellets are about as plump as a rice grain with blunt ends, whereas mouse droppings are slim and short. Tooth marks agree, since rats leave broader and deeper grooves.

  • Burrow openings roughly fist-sized, ringed by smooth packed dirt.
  • Dark grease rubbed along the foot of a wall or pipe.
  • Torn sacks of seed or pet food in a garage or shed.
  • Thudding or dragging overhead once it is dark.

How the work proceeds.

The outdoor lap comes first and records burrows, feeding spots and any gap near the ground. Traps are placed on the runs, tight to walls, beyond the reach of kids and pets. Indoors, the attic and cellar are checked for the entries the lap pointed toward.

A rat entry needs sterner material than a mouse job. Gaps receive heavy mesh, flashing and a hard sealant, never foam or rags, and the repair is verified from both sides. The sealing page explains what goes where.

Rat questions we hear in Melville.

How can I tell rat sign from mouse sign?

Start with size. Rat pellets are roughly as fat as a grain of rice or bigger, where mouse pellets are slimmer and shorter. A heavy thud overhead suggests an animal larger than a mouse.

Do stone walls attract rats?

Not by themselves. The gaps between the stones give animals cover and hide burrow openings. Cutting back brush at the base and looking for new soil does more than tearing the wall apart.

Will rats leave if I wait?

Rarely. Food, warmth and an open doorway keep them around, and unsealed gaps invite them back.

Is it risky to handle what they leave?

Consider droppings, urine and nesting contaminated. Wear gloves and follow the wet-cleanup steps on this page and the CDC links.

Do you deal with burrows in a lawn?

Yes. We find burrow mouths on the outdoor walk, then combine trapping with taking away what feeds and shelters the animals.
