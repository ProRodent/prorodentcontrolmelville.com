Inspect, trap, seal, recheck.

Trapping follows the evidence. Traps go along the walls and routes where droppings and grease smears show the animals travel, matched to mice or rats and kept away from children and pets.

You get a quote after the walk-through, not as a phone guess. After inspecting, we hand over a written scope with a separate price for sealing, for cleanup and for repair.

Calling at 2 a.m.

Describe what you hear and where. A sound in the ceiling above the kitchen points to a different plan than scratching under the basement stairs.

If you see a rodent in living space with children or pets around, call right away. The dispatcher can advise what to do while you wait.

Ahead of the technician's arrival.

  • Keep gnawed cable, nest scraps and droppings out of reach of toddlers and dogs.
  • Open a shed or garage that has sat closed for weeks and let it breathe before you enter.
  • Leave any trap you already set exactly where it sits.
  • Mention any storm debris work, roofing or leaf clearing from recent weeks.

Droppings, minus the broom

These lines boil down published health guidance, and they stay true even if you never book a visit.

  • Filters leak at the microscopic level, so even a good vacuum gets left in the closet.
  • Musty air trapped in a sealed shed is the thing to flush out first, so throw it open and stay clear.
  • Covered hands while you work, clean hands when you finish.
  • Spray until glossy, then walk away briefly.
  • Double-bag when the contents are wet or have sharp edges.
  • If you get sick, say you were near rodents when you see your doctor, because we can speak only to the pest side.
