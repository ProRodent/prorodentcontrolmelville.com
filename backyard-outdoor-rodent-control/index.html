What outdoor rodents are after.

Outdoor control starts with a plain question: what are the animals getting from this yard? Usually the answer is food, shelter or both. Spilled seed, fallen fruit, pet bowls and unsecured trash feed them, while woodpiles, dense ground cover and cluttered sheds hide them.

Rats tend to commit to a spot once they have both. A burrow near a feeder, with a trail to a wall, becomes a highway. Breaking one of the two links often redirects the traffic.

How the outside lap works.

The technician walks the property edge by edge. Burrow mouths show as smooth holes about the width of a fist, often with packed soil and worn grass trails. Fence lines, retaining walls, deck bases and shed floors are checked for gaps and gnawing.

  • Foundation edges: burrows, cracks and vent screens.
  • Fence lines and stone walls: gaps between stones and worn paths.
  • Sheds and decks: floors, steps and storage under them.
  • Gardens and compost: fallen produce, loose seed and open bins.

Lots beside open ground.

Houses near wooded park land, such as the places locals call Arboretum Park and West Hills County Park, may see different animal movement than a block of attached buildings. We cannot say that of any one lot, but if your yard meets trees or open ground, mention it, since the outside walk then covers that edge first.

  • Take photos of burrows, droppings and bits of nest before the weather moves them.

Health agency advice in brief

Going slowly keeps fine particles out of your breathing.

  • Sweeping fast feels efficient and fills the room with drifting specks.
  • Half an hour of open windows is the baseline for a space that was sealed.
  • Skin should not meet droppings, urine or nest material at any point.
  • Every dropping and stain should be dripping, not damp.
  • When the bag is knotted and binned, wash your hands one more time.
  • If you get sick, say you were near rodents when you see your doctor, because we can speak only to the pest side.

Garden and lawn queries.

Will cutting the grass short help?

Somewhat. Tall cover hides runways, so trimmed edges around the foundation help, but food and shelter matter more than grass height.

Do feeders attract rodents?

Often. Seed that lands on the ground feeds rats and mice through the night, so use a catch tray, sweep daily and think about taking feeders indoors at dusk.

Can I fill burrows myself?

Wait until the technician has confirmed no animals are inside. Filling an active burrow only sends the animal to dig another.

What about a compost pile?

A closed bin set well away from the house is much better than an open heap beside the wall.

Do you handle chipmunks or squirrels?

The pages here deal with mice and rats. If another species turns up, the technician says so.
