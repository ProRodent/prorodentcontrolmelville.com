From first call to booking.

The number is (934) 800-1483, and the person who answers will ask a few things. Which street is the building on? What did you see, in which room? How long has it been going on? Are there young children or pets? Your replies decide the crew and the gear we bring.

Midnight calls, Sunday calls and holiday calls all reach a human. When nobody can arrive today, you hear that immediately and receive a window you can plan around.

Inside a visit.

  • A tour from the eaves down through attic, cellar, garage and grounds, looking for fresh evidence.
  • Traps placed along the paths shown by grease smears and droppings.
  • Gaps packed with flashing and woven steel, then topped with a hardening sealant.
  • A return trip to count what was caught, tug on each repair and book cleanout if it is needed.

Sequence is important. If you close holes first, an animal can end up sealed in a wall, and an attic cleaned while its entry points remain open has to be cleaned again.

Claims we leave out.

No founding year, license number, crew portrait, plaque or customer quote appears on this site. Nothing like that was supplied to us in a form we could check, and a made-up detail would only mislead you. Credentials are a fair thing to ask the person at your door.

We also avoid saying rodents will stay away for good. Mature trees, stone walls, big lots and an untidy garage next door keep animals in the area. What we can offer is a thorough inspection, long-lasting materials and a second visit.

Bait, health questions and agency rules.

Federal rules limit how rodent poison may be sold and used, and the EPA link at the bottom of each page gives the overview. We lean toward traps and sealing. If bait is ever used, you will be told the product and the exact spot.

Should droppings need removing, the CDC's order is airing, soaking, wiping, then bagging. Worries about your health should go to a physician, because mice and rats are the limit of our expertise.
