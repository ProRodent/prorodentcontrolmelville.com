When the first trouble shows up.

Cold snaps are the best-known trigger. Once nights drop, mice and rats start testing buildings for warmth, and a loose vent cover or a gap by a pipe becomes an invitation.

Wind and storms can loosen soffits, flashing and vent screens. After a rough night, a quick look around the roofline can save a larger repair later.

As the day firms up.

  • Glove up for anything grimy and scrub your hands well afterward.

Wet methods only

Treat the lines below as a short to-do list for the hour after droppings turn up.

  • Sweeping fast feels efficient and fills the room with drifting specks.
  • Half an hour of open windows is the baseline for a space that was sealed.
  • Leave gloves on until the job is done, then remove them inside out and wash.
  • Spray first, wipe afterward, and let about five minutes pass between the two.
  • Bag it: towels, droppings and any dead animal, picked up with tongs or a gloved hand.
  • Short of breath or running a fever after exposure? Call a doctor.
