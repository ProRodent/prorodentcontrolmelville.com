How seasons drive animals inside.

Leaf drifts and brush piles give shelter all year, but they matter most just before the cold. Moving them away from the wall removes a covered path.

Cold snaps are the best-known trigger. Once nights drop, mice and rats start testing buildings for warmth, and a loose vent cover or a gap by a pipe becomes an invitation.

Calling after hours, on weekends or holidays.

Weekend calls get the same questions as weekday ones: where, since when, and what you have seen. Clear answers move you up the plan quickly.

Scratching at 2 a.m. is a legitimate reason to call. You reach a person, not a recording, and the dispatcher can often schedule the next available visit.

Things to do in the meantime.

  • Move kids and pets to the end of the house furthest from the trouble.
  • Pull on rubber gloves before you handle anything wet, stained or gnawed.
  • Skip glue boards, which catch birds and chipmunks as readily as mice.
  • Photograph the mess before cleaning, because it records the animals' routes.

Health agency advice in brief

These lines boil down published health guidance, and they stay true even if you never book a visit.

  • Put the dustpan down. Sweeping crumbled droppings is how people end up breathing them.
  • Wedge a door, let the breeze run through and look at your watch.
  • Covered hands while you work, clean hands when you finish.
  • Cover the waste and a margin around it with liquid, take a short break, then start wiping.
  • Collect first, carry second, and knot the bag before you walk anywhere.
  • Not feeling well after contact with droppings is a question for a physician.
