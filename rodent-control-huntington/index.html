Why callers ring in.

Not every clue is a mouse in plain sight. Callers from Huntington talk about:

  • Insulation pulled out of a spot in the attic.
  • Shredded paper or fabric tucked in a corner of the garage.
  • Noises that seem to move from the wall to the ceiling.
  • Small tunnels or holes in the lawn beside the foundation.

What a long winter does to a building.

Nights are when rodents travel, so the first sign is often a sound after the television goes off. The season only changes how soon you notice it.

If you hear scratching only on cold nights, the animals are probably using the heat from a furnace or water heater wall. Mention that on the call.

A phone staffed all night.

Describe what you hear and where. A sound in the ceiling above the kitchen points to a different plan than scratching under the basement stairs.

Late-night calls are normal here. People hear noises in the ceiling when everything else is quiet, and the line stays open for exactly that.

Dos and don'ts for droppings

The point of this list is to hold airborne dust down until help arrives.

  • A dry rag is no safer than a broom, since it stirs the same powder.
  • Musty air trapped in a sealed shed is the thing to flush out first, so throw it open and stay clear.
  • Single-use gloves are acceptable, followed by a proper hand wash.
  • Soak, wait, then collect with paper towels.
  • Tie the bag tight and let it ride out with the usual household trash.
  • Diagnosing illness is not our trade, so anyone unwell after contact should consult a clinician.
