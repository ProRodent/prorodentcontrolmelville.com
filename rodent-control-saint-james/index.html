Weather and where rodents go.

Cold snaps are the best-known trigger. Once nights drop, mice and rats start testing buildings for warmth, and a loose vent cover or a gap by a pipe becomes an invitation.

Walk the outside of the house in early fall and again after the first hard freeze. Look for fresh soil, loosened covers and leaves piled against siding.

Why callers ring in.

People in Saint James usually call after noticing one of these:

  • Scratching in the ceiling or wall when the house is quiet.
  • Tracks in dust on a basement shelf or the attic floor.
  • A cabinet door that gets pushed open from behind.
  • Dark oily rubs on a baseboard or pipe.

A quick damp-cleaning guide

Going slowly keeps fine particles out of your breathing.

  • Scrub lightly. Heavy elbow work throws dust and defeats the purpose of wetting.
  • Closed rooms keep their air, so let a shut-up space exchange it before you walk in.
  • Skip bare hands entirely, because waste on a fingertip can end up in a mouth.
  • Soak, wait, then collect with paper towels.
  • Collect first, carry second, and knot the bag before you walk anywhere.
  • Short of breath or running a fever after exposure? Call a doctor.
